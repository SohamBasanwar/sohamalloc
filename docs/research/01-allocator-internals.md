## 1. The design split that matters most

For small objects, every allocator falls into one of three families:

1. **Owner-returns** (mimalloc, snmalloc, rpmalloc). A thread-owned heap owns each page or slab. A free from another thread has to send the block back to that owner: one atomic push per object in mimalloc and rpmalloc, or batched messages in snmalloc. Pages fill and drain together, so whole pages can go back to the OS. The costs are an atomic per remote free, or buffering delay, and an idle owner can strand memory unless it has an abandon/reclaim protocol.
2. **Ownership-free caches** (glibc tcache, jemalloc tcache, TCMalloc per-CPU slabs, Scudo TSD caches, PartitionAlloc thread cache). `free` pushes any pointer into the local cache, whoever allocated it. The owning structure is touched only on flush, under a lock. This keeps the free path free of atomics. The cost is that objects migrate between threads and CPUs and mix up pages, so locality depends on the flush policy (see jemalloc's locality-aware GC below).
3. **No cache** (hardened_malloc). Every operation takes a lock per arena and size class, so the allocator always knows exactly which slots are free. That is what makes quarantine and randomization sound ([README](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/README.md)).

## 2. Allocator by allocator

### 2.1 mimalloc v3 (`dev3`)

- **Size classes.** There are 73 bins ([types.h](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/include/mimalloc/types.h)). Below 64 B the step is one word; above that, `mi_bin()` takes the highest bit of the word size plus the next two bits, giving 4 bins per doubling ([page-queue.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/alloc/page-queue.c), table in [init.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/heap/init.c)). With 16-byte alignment, words are rounded to pairs, so there are no 24/40/56-byte classes. The source comment says "~12.5% worst internal fragmentation". My replication of `mi_bin()` gives a worst case of 25.0% just above each power of two (for example 262145→327680) **[derived]**.
- **Page kinds.** Small pages are 64 KiB (objects up to about 10 KiB), medium pages 512 KiB, and large pages 4 MiB (objects up to 512 KiB). Above that, each object gets its own "singleton" page. Arenas are bitmaps of 64 KiB slices grouped into 512-bit "bchunks" ([types.h](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/include/mimalloc/types.h)). Arenas are reserved 1 GiB at a time by default, with at most 160 arenas ([options.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/util/options.c)).
- **malloc fast path.** The thread's `theap->pages_free_direct[wsize]` (for sizes up to 1 KiB) gives a page. The code pops `page->free` and does `xused += 0x10001`, which updates a 16-bit used count and a 16-bit alloc count packed into one word with a single add. A NULL free list tail-calls `_mi_malloc_generic`. The source describes this as "about 7 to 10 instructions with a single test" ([alloc.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/alloc/alloc.c)). The default theap points to a static empty theap, so the fast path needs no init check ([types.h](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/include/mimalloc/types.h)).
- **free: finding the metadata.** In the default non-secure build, page metadata sits at the 256 MiB-aligned start of each arena chunk. The page is computed as `align_down(p, 256MiB) | ((p/64KiB) % 4096) * sizeof(mi_page_t)`, followed by one load of `page->self`. No page-map walk is needed. Checked frees (`MI_SECURE`) use a 2-level page map instead: 4 MiB of top-level reservation, with submaps of 8192 entries each covering 512 MiB. Systems with at most 40 VA bits use a flat map with 1 byte per slice ([page-map.h](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/include/mimalloc/heap/page-map.h)).
- **free: the ownership test.** `xtid = thread_id ^ page->xthread_id`, where the low 2 bits of `xthread_id` hold the "in full queue" and "has interior pointers" flags. `xtid==0` means local, not full and aligned, so the block is pushed onto `local_free`. `xtid<=3` means local but special. Low bits equal to zero means a plain remote free. Everything else is generic ([free.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/alloc/free.c)).
- **Free lists.** Each page has three intrusive lists. `free` is what `malloc` pops. `local_free` is a deferred local list that migrates to `free` only when `free` empties, which gives a "monotonic heartbeat" for collection. `xthread_free` holds frees from other threads, and its low bit is an *ownership* bit ([types.h](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/include/mimalloc/types.h)).
- **Remote free.** The freeing thread pushes the block onto `xthread_free` with a CAS loop. If the push also flips the owned bit, the page was abandoned, and the freeing thread now owns it and may reclaim it (`page_reclaim_on_free=1`) ([free.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/alloc/free.c), [options.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/util/options.c)). There is no batching.
- **Fragmentation heuristic.** When the current page runs dry, the generic path scans up to `page_max_candidates=4` pages. It prefers the one with the higher used count that is not "mostly used", and frees fully empty candidates along the way ([alloc-generic.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/alloc/alloc-generic.c)).
- **OS layer.** Purging happens after `purge_delay=1000` ms, multiplied by `arena_purge_mult=4` for arenas. It uses `MADV_DONTNEED`, chosen because it "decreases rss immediately", rather than `MADV_FREE` ([prim.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/prim/unix/prim.c), [readme](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/readme.md)). VMAs are named "mimalloc" through `PR_SET_VMA_ANON_NAME`. `MAP_NORESERVE` is used, and 1 GiB hugetlb pages can be reserved.
- **Huge pages.** On Linux the CMake `DEFAULT` resolves to `FULL`, which sets `allow_thp=2` and a 2 MiB minimum purge size so that purging does not split THPs ([CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/CMakeLists.txt), [options.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/util/options.c)). `allow_thp=0` calls `PR_SET_THP_DISABLE`.
- **Fork, threads, init.** There is only a child-side `pthread_atfork` handler. The source states that "fork() is only safe if no other thread is executing inside mimalloc" ([subproc.c](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/src/heap/subproc.c)). When a thread exits, its theap pages are abandoned into per-bin arena bitmaps for other threads to reclaim.
- **Admitted weaknesses.**
  - Issue [#1104](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/issues/1104): v3 is several times slower than v2 on multithreaded alloc/free (one report: 0.62 s vs 4.96 s), with heavy futex and system time. The maintainer found singleton pages shared across threads, `rep stosb` being slow at 128 B and above, and finer-grained commits costing about 20%. The issue was still open in the part I read.
  - Issue [#1282](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/issues/1282): the 2 MiB THP purge minimum raised VmRSS by about 81% (26→47 MB), and `MIMALLOC_ALLOW_THP=0` fixed it.
  - A `types.h` comment admits that 64–512 KiB "large pages" can leave many partially used 4 MiB pages.
  - The 2026 readme lists many bug-fix releases from "LLM audit[s]" ([readme](https://github.com/microsoft/mimalloc/blob/dev3/readme.md)), a sign that v3 is still maturing.

### 2.2 TCMalloc (google/tcmalloc)

- **Size classes.** `class_array_` is indexed by `(s+7)/8` for s ≤ 1024 and by `(s+127+(120<<7))/128` up to `kMaxSize`. These two logical arrays are flattened into one, with extra copies for hot/cold, `new` vs `malloc`, security partitions and NUMA. Choosing a partition is an add, not a branch ([sizemap.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/sizemap.h)). The default build (8 KiB pages) has 85 base classes and `kMaxSize` = 256 KiB ([common.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/common.h)). The class table is annotated with fleet usage data ("used" GiB per class) ([size_classes.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/size_classes.cc)).
- **malloc fast path.** `GetSizeClass`, then a sampler byte countdown (`TryRecordAllocationFast`), then a per-CPU rseq pop. Anything else tail-calls (`TCMALLOC_MUSTTAIL`, `SLOW_PATH_BARRIER`) ([tcmalloc.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/tcmalloc.cc)). The pop reads `header[class].current` (16-bit `current`/`end`) and loads the slot. A low-bit sentinel in the slot marks underflow. It prefetches the next object, and a single store to `current` commits the operation ([docs/rseq.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/rseq.md)).
- **free fast path.** One mask test covers bits above 48, misalignment and a 3-bit memory tag at bit `min(kAddressBits-4, 42)`. It sends NULL, sampled, cold and ill-formed pointers off the fast path ([memory_tag.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/internal/memory_tag.h), [tcmalloc.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/tcmalloc.cc)). An unsized free then reads a 3-level radix page map. Its 8-byte `PageMeta` keeps the size class in the low bits, so one load yields it ([pagemap.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/pagemap.h)). A sized delete computes the class from the size and skips the page map entirely.
- **Free-list representation.** Per-CPU slabs are arrays of pointers. Central spans store free objects as 16-bit indices, either in a 12-entry embedded cache or as a bitmap for spans with few objects, and use reciprocal multiplication instead of division ([span.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/span.h)).
- **Cross-thread frees.** TCMalloc tracks no ownership. Overflow goes to a transfer cache, sharded per L3 when there are at least 3 L3 domains ([transfer_cache.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/transfer_cache.h)). From there it goes to a central free list that keeps spans in 8 lists ranked by allocated count ([central_freelist.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/central_freelist.h)).
- **Cache sizing.**
  - The default per-CPU cache limit is 1.5 MiB ([common.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/common.h)).
  - Dynamic slabs grow at 0.9 and shrink at 0.4 occupancy ([parameters.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/parameters.cc)).
  - Capacity moves from lightly used CPU caches to heavily used ones based on miss rate, and a heavily used cache may grow past the limit ([tuning.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/tuning.md)).
  - Idle CPU caches are drained every 30 background sleep periods ([background.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/background.cc)).
  - In "virtual CPU" mode, slabs are indexed by rseq `mm_cid` instead of the CPU number ([percpu.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/internal/percpu.cc)).
- **Page heap (Temeraire).** The filler packs spans smaller than a hugepage onto hugepages, keeping sparsely and densely accessed spans apart ([huge_page_filler.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/huge_page_filler.h)). HugeRegion lets allocations of several hugepages share their slack ([regions-are-not-optional.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/regions-are-not-optional.md)). A HugeCache holds whole hugepages ([temeraire.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/temeraire.md)). The cold heap is marked `MADV_NOHUGEPAGE`. `MADV_COLLAPSE` is backed off when its latency is high. "Skip-subrelease" uses 10 s and 120 s demand windows ([parameters.cc](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/parameters.cc)). OSDI'21 reports 7.7% more RPS and 2.4% less RAM across 8 applications, and in the fleet 6% fewer TLB stalls and 26% less fragmentation ([pub50370](https://research.google/pubs/pub50370/)). The ASPLOS'24 study reports +1.4% throughput and −3.4% RAM fleet-wide from cache sizing, topology awareness and better packing ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/characterizing-a-memory-allocator-at-warehouse-scale/)).
- **Release to the OS.** The background release rate defaults to **0** in the OSS `parameters.cc`. The background loop runs only if the application calls `ProcessBackgroundActions()`, which "does not return" ([malloc_extension.h](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/tcmalloc/malloc_extension.h)).
- **Extras.** Sampling every 2 MiB; GWP-ASan; size-returning `__size_returning_new` and `alloc_at_least`. Sized-delete mismatches are checked lazily, when objects reach the central free list ([mismatched-sized-delete.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/mismatched-sized-delete.md)). An experimental lifetime-based allocator predicts short- or long-lived large objects from the stack trace, and has a *counterfactual* mode that only measures ([lifetime-based-allocator.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/lifetime-based-allocator.md)).
- **Fork and environment.** A grep of the tree finds no `pthread_atfork`. The docs state that TCMalloc is built and tested with THP=`always` and `overcommit_memory=1` ([tuning.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/tuning.md)).
- **Admitted weaknesses.**
  - glibc 2.35+ registers rseq itself, so users must set `GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.pthread.rseq=0` or TCMalloc silently falls back to per-thread caches ([MongoDB SERVER-90036](https://jira.mongodb.com/browse/SERVER-90036)).
  - TCMalloc overlays its slab pointer onto `cpu_id_start` and relies on the kernel rewriting that field on every return to userspace. Linux 6.19 stopped doing so, which broke it. Linus Torvalds ruled that a regression that must be fixed, and the likely fix restores the old behavior only for legacy 32-byte rseq registrations ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1070072/)).
  - The cached memory limit is per CPU, so total cached memory grows with the number of CPUs ([design.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/design.md)).

### 2.3 jemalloc (`dev`)

- **Size classes.** 4 classes per doubling (`SC_LG_NGROUP=2`), 8-byte tiny classes, and a lookup table up to 4 KiB (`SC_LG_MAX_LOOKUP=12`) ([sc.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/sc.h)).
- **free fast path.** `tsd_get`. For an unsized free, an rtree lookup goes through the per-thread `rtree_ctx` cache (16 direct-mapped entries plus an 8-entry L2) and returns `{szind, slab}` without touching the extent ([emap.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/emap.h), [rtree_tsd.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/rtree_tsd.h)). For a sized free up to 4 KiB, size maps straight to the index with no rtree access. A single compare, `deallocated+usize >= threshold`, covers "TSD uninitialized or not nominal" plus GC and profiling events. Then `cache_bin_dalloc_easy` pushes onto an array stack ([jemalloc_internal_inlines_c.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/jemalloc_internal_inlines_c.h)).
- **rtree.** 2 levels for 48-bit VA with 4 KiB pages. The leaf is compact, with `szind` packed into the unused high pointer bits ([rtree.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/rtree.h)). A `DYNAMIC_PAGE_SIZE` build mode exists.
- **tcache.**
  - Bins are pointer arrays. `tcache_max` is 32 KiB, and an incremental GC runs every 64 KiB allocated ([tcache.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/tcache.c)).
  - Fill counts double after a refill and halve after underuse; retention follows the low-water mark ([tcache_ncached_target.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/tcache_ncached_target.h)).
  - GC is **locality-aware**: it prefers to flush pointers outside the current slab or outside a ±2 MiB neighborhood (`TCACHE_GC_NEIGHBOR_LIMIT`) ([tcache.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/tcache.c), [tcache.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/tcache.h)).
  - Remote objects sit in the local tcache until a flush takes the owning bin's lock.
- **Arenas and threads.** The default is `narenas = 4 × ncpus` ([jemalloc.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/jemalloc.c)). `percpu_arena` is disabled ([percpu_arena.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/percpu_arena.h)), and so is `background_thread` ([background_thread.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/background_thread.c)). On fork, the prefork handler locks every mutex ([jemalloc_fork.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/jemalloc_fork.c)).
- **OS layer.** Decay purging defaults to `dirty_decay_ms` = 10 s and `muzzy_decay_ms` = 0 ([arena.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/arena.h)). The HPA is **off** by default (`opt_hpa=false`). Its defaults are a 64 KiB maximum slab allocation, a hugification threshold of 95% of a hugepage, `dirty_mult` 25%, `hugify_delay` 10 s, a 5 s minimum purge interval and `hugify_style_lazy`, with styles none, eager and lazy documented ([hpa_opts.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/hpa_opts.h)). Purges are **batched** through `process_madvise(PIDFD_SELF, iovec, MADV_DONTNEED)`, with a gate that falls back on `EPERM`, `ENOSYS` and similar errors. `MADV_COLLAPSE` is supported ([pages.c](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/src/pages.c)).
- **Admitted weaknesses.**
  - Evans' postmortem says the HPA "recently cratered" from "tweaks piled on top of each other without the requisite refactoring", and estimates "hundreds of hours of refactoring" of debt. He also regrets removing Valgrind support ([postmortem](https://jasone.github.io/2025/06/12/jemalloc-postmortem/)).
  - TUNING.md admits that a high arena count "may also increase overall memory fragmentation" ([TUNING.md](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/TUNING.md)).
  - The repository was archived in June 2025 and unarchived by Meta in March 2026. Meta's stated focus is HPA, packing, caching, purging and AArch64 ([Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/03/02/data-infrastructure/investing-in-infrastructure-metas-renewed-commitment-to-jemalloc/)).

### 2.4 glibc ptmalloc2 (master)

- **Metadata.** Each chunk has an inline header holding the size plus flag bits. A chunk outside the main arena finds its arena with `heap_for_ptr`, which aligns the pointer down to `HEAP_MAX_SIZE` (2 × `DEFAULT_MMAP_THRESHOLD_MAX`, i.e. 64 MiB on 64-bit) ([arena.c](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=malloc/arena.c;hb=HEAD), [malloc.c](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=malloc/malloc.c;hb=HEAD)).
- **tcache.**
  - 64 small bins in 16-byte steps, up to 1032 B usable, plus 12 large bins up to 4 MiB that are enabled by raising `glibc.malloc.tcache_max` (since 2.42) ([NEWS](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=NEWS;hb=HEAD)).
  - `TCACHE_FILL_COUNT` is 16 in master.
  - A double free is detected with `e->key == tcache_key`. Safe-linking applies `PROTECT_PTR(pos,ptr) = (pos>>12) ^ ptr` ([malloc.c](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=malloc/malloc.c;hb=HEAD)).
- **Fastbins are gone from master.** There is no fastbin code, and `do_set_mxfast()` is a no-op. **[UNVERIFIED: which release and which commit removed them.]**
- **Free path.** The tcache accepts chunks from any arena. On overflow, `_int_free_chunk(arena_for_chunk(p))` takes the owning arena's lock and coalesces.
- **Arena cap.** Master caps arenas at `max(nprocs, arena_test=8)`. Older guidance was 8 × cores, and a patch titled "malloc: Reduce maximum arenas" proposed `max(8, ncores)` ([patchwork](https://patchwork.sourceware.org/project/glibc/patch/PAWPR08MB89821FAE648721A1627D33C68351A@PAWPR08MB8982.eurprd08.prod.outlook.com/)). **[UNVERIFIED: release.]**
- **OS layer.** The main heap uses brk; other arenas use 64 MiB mmap'd heaps. The mmap threshold is dynamic between 128 KiB and 32 MiB. THP comes through `glibc.malloc.hugetlb`, and THP has been on by default on AArch64 since 2.43 ([NEWS](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=NEWS;hb=HEAD)).
- **Recent API changes.** C23 `free_sized` arrived in 2.43, but `__free_sized` marks its size parameter `__attribute_maybe_unused__` ([malloc.c](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=malloc/malloc.c;hb=HEAD)). The 2.45 development NEWS removes glibc's `--enable-memory-tagging` configure option (the AArch64 MTE support) ([NEWS](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=NEWS;hb=HEAD)).
- **Admitted weaknesses.** Arena proliferation spreads fragmentation and cannot be fully capped ([bug 11261](https://sourceware.org/bugzilla/show_bug.cgi?id=11261)). `malloc_trim` did not work on non-main arenas until bug 30769 was fixed in 2.44 ([NEWS](https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=NEWS;hb=HEAD)).

### 2.5 snmalloc

- **Size classes.** Exponent-mantissa with 2 intermediate bits (4 per doubling), a 16 B minimum step, small objects up to 64 KiB and 16 KiB minimum slabs ([sizeclassconfig.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/ds_core/sizeclassconfig.h)). Each class stores a `div_mult` and a `mod_zero_mult`, so "index of the object containing p" and "is p the start of an object" each cost one multiply. That enables O(1) interior-pointer and bounds checks ([sizeclasstable.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/ds/sizeclasstable.h)).
- **Metadata and the free test.** A *flat* page map has one entry per 16 KiB chunk, reserved for the whole address space and committed lazily. Before initialization the body points to one static default entry, so `free(NULL)` and `malloc_usable_size` work with no init check ([ds/pagemap.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/ds/pagemap.h)). On free, `entry.get_remote() == public_state()` sends the block down the local path; otherwise it goes to `dealloc_remote` ([corealloc.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/mem/corealloc.h)).
- **Free lists.** The forward edge is encoded and the backward edge is signed with `prev_encoded = f(curr,next,key)`. Randomization comes from building two lists at once and choosing between them at random ([freelist.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/mem/freelist.h)). The paper adds that its bump-pointer free list needs only 64 bits of metadata per slab ([paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2020/04/snmalloc.pdf)).
- **Remote frees.** Each thread keeps a cache with 256 destination slots and flushes it once 16 KiB has accumulated (`REMOTE_CACHE = MIN_CHUNK_SIZE`). Same-slab batching rings (2-way × 8 sets) come from BatchIt. The receiver processes at most 1 MiB per batch ([allocconfig.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/mitigations/allocconfig.h), [remotecache.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/mem/remotecache.h)). Enqueueing on the MPSC queue takes one fence and one atomic exchange ([paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2020/04/snmalloc.pdf)). BatchIt reports gains above 20% on some producer-consumer workloads ([BatchIt](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/batchit-optimizing-message-passing-allocators-for-producer-consumer-workloads-an-intellectual-abstract/)).
- **Backend.** Requests pass through a chain of ranges: a per-thread small buddy, a per-thread large buddy refilled in 2 MiB pieces ("typical superpage size"), a commit range, a global lock, a global large buddy refilled in 16 MiB pieces, and finally the PAL ([AddressSpace.md](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/docs/AddressSpace.md)). A chunk returned to the backend is decommitted immediately with `MADV_FREE`, falling back to `MADV_DONTNEED` ([pal_linux.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/pal/pal_linux.h), [commitrange.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/backend_helpers/commitrange.h)).
- **Fork.** `PreventFork` makes `fork()` wait until no thread is inside an allocator critical section, using an atfork handler plus a counter ([prevent_fork.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/ds_aal/prevent_fork.h)).
- **Security.** A bitset of mitigations: free-list forward and backward edges, `random_initial`/`preserve`/`extra_slab`, metadata protection, `pal_enforce_access` and others ([mitigations.h](https://github.com/microsoft/snmalloc/blob/main/src/snmalloc/mitigations/mitigations.h)).

### 2.6 Scudo (LLVM)

- **Chunk header.** 8 bytes packing ClassId:8, State:2 (Available/Allocated/Quarantined), Origin:2 (malloc/new/new[]/memalign), SizeOrUnusedBytes:20, Offset:16 and a 16-bit CRC32 checksum. It catches new/free mismatches and size or alignment mismatches ([chunk.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/chunk.h), [allocator_config.def](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/allocator_config.def)).
- **Primary64.** One region per class, so the class falls out of `(p - base) >> RegionSizeLog` with no page map. Regions start at a random offset, are mapped incrementally, have their blocks shuffled and are **never unmapped**, only released ([primary64.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/primary64.h)). On Android: 256 MiB regions (`RegionSizeLog` 28), 1 MiB groups, 256 KiB map increments, **32-bit compact pointers** and a 1 s release interval ([allocator_config.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/allocator_config.h)).
- **Caches.** TSDs are either exclusive or a shared pool; Android uses a shared pool of at most 8, default 2. A thread trylocks its current TSD and falls to a slow path if that fails ([tsd_shared.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/tsd_shared.h)).
- **Size classes.** On Android they are an **explicit 32-entry table** ([size_class_map.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/compiler-rt/lib/scudo/standalone/size_class_map.h)). The secondary allocator uses mmap with a cache: 32 entries, at most 2 MiB each and at most 14 MiB resident.
- **Admitted weaknesses.** A 2020 Samsung report found Geekbench multi-core scores up to about 2× lower than jemalloc ([llvm-bugs](https://lists.llvm.org/pipermail/llvm-bugs/2020-July/085015.html)). A 2024 Google talk claims the Geekbench gap is under 1% and memory use is lower ([slides](https://www-new.llvm.org/devmtg/2024-10/slides/quicktalks/Duan-Ferris-WhyYouShouldUseScudo.pdf)).

### 2.7 hardened_malloc (GrapheneOS)

- **Size classes.** A table: 16 B steps up to 128 B, then 4 per doubling up to 16 KiB, or 128 KiB with extended classes. Slot counts per slab are tuned per class ([h_malloc.c](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/h_malloc.c)).
- **Metadata.** `slab_size_class(p)` is plain arithmetic: (p − region start) / `ARENA_SIZE` gives the arena, and the remainder / class region size gives the class. Each class region is 32 GiB, there are 4 arenas, and all metadata is out of line ([config/default.mk](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/config/default.mk)).
- **Defaults.** Zero on free, a write-after-free check, slot randomization, canaries, a guard slab after every slab, a slab quarantine, and a large-region quarantine (random 256, queue 1024).
- **Design choices.** There is no thread cache, deliberately ("legacy design choice", [README](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/README.md)). Purging uses `MADV_DONTNEED_LOCKED` and treats failure as fatal ([memory.c](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/memory.c)), so it needs a kernel that supports that flag (5.18+ **[UNVERIFIED]**). On fork, the atfork handler takes every lock (`full_lock`). A `constructor(101)` runs initialization early.
- **Kernel wishlist.** The maintainers' wishlist asks for cheap guard pages and a higher VMA limit ([KERNEL_FEATURE_WISHLIST.md](https://github.com/GrapheneOS/hardened_malloc/blob/main/KERNEL_FEATURE_WISHLIST.md)). Linux 6.13 added `MADV_GUARD_INSTALL`/`REMOVE`, which install guards as page-table markers with no VMA ([LKML](https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/2410.2/11241.html)).

### 2.8 PartitionAlloc (Chromium)

- **Address space.** Memory comes in 2 MiB super pages inside *pools*, and membership in a pool is a single mask test ([glossary.md](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/glossary.md)).
- **Super-page layout.** Partition pages are 16 KiB, and the first and last partition page of each super page are guard pages. Metadata, 32 B per partition page, lives in a system page inside the first guard partition page. Slot spans are at most 4 partition pages and are sized to end exactly on a system-page boundary ([PartitionAlloc.md](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/PartitionAlloc.md), [constants](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/partition_alloc_constants.h)).
- **Buckets.** A clz-based index gives 8 buckets per order ("denser"); `index |= 1` turns that into 4 per order ("neutral"). Sizes are bucketed up to `kMaxBucketedOrder=20`, i.e. just under 1 MiB ([bucket_lookup.h](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/bucket_lookup.h)).
- **Free lists.** The next pointer is **byte-swapped**, which makes it non-canonical if dereferenced and defeats partial overwrites. An inverted shadow copy sits beside it, and `IsWellFormed` checks that next stays in the same super page and outside the metadata ([encoded_next_freelist.h](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/encoded_next_freelist.h), [partition_freelist_entry.h](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/partition_freelist_entry.h)). Slots are provisioned one system page at a time so untouched memory is never dirtied.
- **Admitted weaknesses.** The design doc says there is "no mechanism … to prioritize selection of a slot span based on the number of already allocated slots". The thread-cache purge interval adapts, halving when a lot is cached and doubling when little is, but the source admits it cannot purge the caches of sleeping threads ([thread_cache.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/thread_cache.cc)).

### 2.9 rpmalloc (`develop`)

- **Layout.** Spans are 256 MiB and aligned, so `block & SPAN_MASK` gives the span header with no map. Pages are 64 KiB, 1 MiB, 4 MiB or 16 MiB, each with a 128 B inline header. Small classes step by 16 B up to 4 KiB, with 12 classes per tier above that, up to 2 MiB ([rpmalloc.c](https://github.com/mjansson/rpmalloc/blob/develop/rpmalloc/rpmalloc.c), [README](https://github.com/mjansson/rpmalloc/blob/develop/README.md)).
- **free.** A single field, `page->heap_fast == thread_heap`, says "owned here, not full, no aligned blocks". If it matches, the block is pushed onto `local_free`; otherwise it goes on a per-page atomic `thread_free` list.
- **Admitted weaknesses.** Decommit and recommit thrash when a thread's high and low water marks differ by more than the retention thresholds. Spans are aligned by over-mapping and trimming the excess ([README](https://github.com/mjansson/rpmalloc/blob/develop/README.md)).

## 4. Ideas worth stealing

1. **One-compare ownership test.** Put flags in the low bits of the owner id and XOR it with your own id; zero means the plain local fast path (mimalloc `xthread_id`, rpmalloc `heap_fast`).
2. **Metadata found by arithmetic on the hot path, a page map for validation.** mimalloc keeps both an aligned-metadata path and a checked page-map path, and selects between them by build mode. Scudo, hardened_malloc and rpmalloc go further and need no page map at all. PartitionAlloc masks to the super page and keeps one table entry per super page only to locate direct-map reservations ([reservation_offset_table.h](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/base/allocator/partition_allocator/src/partition_alloc/reservation_offset_table.h)).
3. **Address-space tagging.** TCMalloc's tag bits let a single mask test reject NULL, wild, sampled and cold pointers. PartitionAlloc's pools test membership with one mask.
4. **A flat page map whose default entry is a static sentinel**, so `free(NULL)` and calls before initialization need no branch (snmalloc).
5. **Fold rare conditions into one threshold compare.** jemalloc's `deallocated+usize >= threshold` covers TSD state, GC and profiling at once; mimalloc's sample countdown and TCMalloc's sampler countdown do the same for sampling.
6. **Pack counters so one add updates two of them** (mimalloc `xused += 0x10001`).
7. **Batch remote frees by destination and by slab** (snmalloc with BatchIt) rather than paying one CAS per object (mimalloc). Combine this with mimalloc's **owner bit in the thread-free head**, so a free can atomically take over an abandoned page.
8. **Adaptive cache sizing.** jemalloc doubles and halves fill counts and keeps the low-water mark. TCMalloc grows and shrinks slabs at 0.9/0.4 and moves capacity between CPUs by miss rate.
9. **Locality-aware flushing**, keeping same-slab and ±2 MiB neighbours (jemalloc).
10. **Rank by occupancy everywhere.** Central spans in 8 occupancy lists (TCMalloc); prefer the fullest page up to 4 candidates (mimalloc); keep sparse and dense spans on separate hugepages and use HugeRegion for the slack of large allocations (Temeraire).
11. **Provision lazily:** chain free slots one system page at a time (PartitionAlloc).
12. **Cheap free-list hardening.** Byte-swap plus a shadow copy and structural checks (PartitionAlloc); safe-linking (glibc); signed back-edges (snmalloc).
13. **Compact 32-bit pointers in caches and batches** to halve cache metadata (Scudo).
14. **Derive size classes from data**, from fleet profiles (TCMalloc) or traces (Scudo Android), and use reciprocal multiplication for O(1) interior-pointer and bounds math (snmalloc).
15. **Hints with zero fast-path cost.** Hot/cold, `new`/`malloc` and security partitions become array offsets (TCMalloc). Return the real size through size-returning APIs (`__size_returning_new`) and C23 `free_sized`, and validate sized delete lazily, in batches.
16. **Prediction with a counterfactual mode** that measures before it acts (TCMalloc's lifetime allocator), which suits sohamalloc's lifetime-placement bet.
17. **Kernel-friendly batching.** `process_madvise(PIDFD_SELF)` with a fallback gate (jemalloc); `MADV_COLLAPSE` with latency backoff (TCMalloc); `MADV_NOHUGEPAGE` for cold data; `PR_SET_VMA_ANON_NAME` labels (mimalloc and snmalloc); `MADV_GUARD_INSTALL` for VMA-free guard pages ([LKML](https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/2410.2/11241.html)).
18. **Fork that waits for allocator critical sections to drain** (snmalloc `PreventFork`), instead of locking every mutex (jemalloc, hardened_malloc) or documenting fork as unsafe (mimalloc).
19. **rseq per-CPU slabs with a single committing store, indexed by `mm_cid`**, so cache memory scales with concurrently running threads rather than with CPUs (TCMalloc's kMM mode).

## 5. Pitfalls to avoid

1. **Do not depend on undocumented kernel or libc behavior.** TCMalloc's use of `cpu_id_start` broke on Linux 6.19, and its own rseq registration conflicts with glibc's ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1070072/)). Use glibc's registered rseq area (`__rseq_offset`), and fall back gracefully when it is absent.
2. **A fixed THP policy loses either way.** A 2 MiB purge minimum cost about 81% RSS in mimalloc #1282, while splitting hugepages hurts the TLB. Decide per hugepage from occupancy, and know that the kernel's underused-THP shrinker splits huge pages whose zero-filled page count exceeds `max_ptes_none` ([kernel docs](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/transhuge.html)).
3. **`MADV_FREE` leaves RSS looking high** until the kernel reclaims the pages, and containers act on RSS (snmalloc's default). mimalloc deliberately uses `DONTNEED`.
4. **Do not make memory return depend on the application.** TCMalloc needs a user thread calling `ProcessBackgroundActions`; jemalloc's `background_thread` is off by default; PartitionAlloc cannot purge sleeping threads. Idle processes and idle threads strand memory.
5. **Per-CPU and per-arena caches scale with core count:** 1.5 MiB per CPU in TCMalloc, 4 × ncpu arenas in jemalloc, and glibc's history of arena-driven fragmentation ([bug 11261](https://sourceware.org/bugzilla/show_bug.cgi?id=11261)). Cap them by the allowed CPUs and cgroup limit, not by the host's core count.
6. **Initial-exec TLS breaks `dlopen`** ("cannot allocate memory in static TLS block", [RH 1483558](https://bugzilla.redhat.com/show_bug.cgi?id=1483558), [ARROW-17246](https://jira.apache.org/jira/browse/ARROW-17246)).
7. **The zeroing and commit paths matter.** `rep stosb` versus `memset`, and fine-grained commits, made mimalloc v3 slower on calloc-heavy loads (#1104).
8. **Sharing singleton or large pages across threads** hurt mimalloc v3's cache behavior (#1104).
9. **Technical debt in the hugepage allocator** sank jemalloc's HPA. Design it cleanly, with tests and a counterfactual mode, from day one ([postmortem](https://jasone.github.io/2025/06/12/jemalloc-postmortem/)).
10. **Keep Valgrind, ASan and tool hooks.** Removing them alienated jemalloc users.
11. **Benchmark hygiene.** mimalloc-bench's "tc" is gperftools, not Google's TCMalloc. TCMalloc itself is tuned under THP=always and overcommit=1 ([tuning.md](https://github.com/google/tcmalloc/blob/master/docs/tuning.md)).
12. **Detect the page size at runtime.** arm64 kernels may use 16K or 64K pages, which is why jemalloc's rtree has a `DYNAMIC_PAGE_SIZE` mode ([rtree.h](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/blob/dev/include/jemalloc/internal/rtree.h)).
13. **Do not trust size hints.** glibc ignores the `free_sized` size, so callers' sizes are never tested there. Check them lazily (TCMalloc).
14. **Security without caches costs 2–4× in time [derived].** Tier it rather than making it all-or-nothing.

## 6. Faults in this research and open questions

- Sources move daily; mimalloc and rpmalloc had commits on the day I read them. Pin these commits before relying on any line-level claim.
- Several "defaults" are build-dependent: mimalloc's THP default depends on CMake, TCMalloc's on page shift, and the OSS release rate of 0 may differ inside Google.
- I did not verify the glibc fastbin-removal commit, the release that changed the arena cap, or the kernel version that first shipped `PIDFD_SELF` (the v7 series was posted in January 2025, [LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2501.3/06907.html)).
- PartitionAlloc's `FromAddr(addr, root)` overloads hint that metadata may be moving out of the super page. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- I did not examine Scudo's and PartitionAlloc's hugepage handling.
- The derived calibration numbers are geometric means from a single machine and hide per-benchmark tails, for example on larson and xmalloc. sohamalloc needs its own multi-machine harness.
